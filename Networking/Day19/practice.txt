Q1. How many bits are in an IPv4 address?

Answer:
32 bits


Q2. How many bits are in each IPv4 octet?

Answer:
8 bits


Q3. For /26, how many host bits are there?

32 - 26 = 6

Answer:
6 host bits


Q4. How many total addresses are in a /26?

2^6 = 64

Answer:
64 addresses


Q5. How many usable hosts are in a /26?

64 - 2 = 62

Answer:
62 usable hosts


Q6. How many total addresses are in a /27?

2^5 = 32

Answer:
32 addresses


Q7. How many usable hosts are in a /27?

32 - 2 = 30

Answer:
30 usable hosts

----------------------------------------
IMPORTANT CONCEPT
----------------------------------------

An IP such as:

192.168.50.130/26

does NOT mean that 130 becomes 128.

130 is the actual host IP.

128 is the NETWORK ADDRESS of the subnet
that contains 130.

So:

Network:
192.168.50.128

Host:
192.168.50.130

Broadcast:
192.168.50.191
